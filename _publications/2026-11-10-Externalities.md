# highlights:
#   - Introduced the concept of cross-attribute externalities, showing how fairness interventions targeting one sensitive attribute can affect disparities associated with others.
#   - Developed a Wasserstein-based distributional framework to characterize changes in both marginal and joint fairness measures.
#   - Derived explicit trajectories for fairness disparities under jointly Gaussian models, identifying conditions under which interventions improve target fairness while worsening fairness elsewhere.
#   - Established sharp partial-identification bounds for cross-attribute externalities when sensitive attributes are unavailable.
#   - Illustrated the phenomenon through classification and regression experiments, including applications to the ACSIncome dataset.

This paper investigates how fairness interventions targeting one sensitive attribute can unintentionally exacerbate disparities associated with other, potentially unobserved, attributes. It develops a Wasserstein-based framework to characterize and quantify these cross-attribute externalities.
